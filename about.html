About the museum

A living map of Beijing, written by the people who walk it.

Beijing is photographed millions of times a day, and almost all of those photos sink into private albums. The Living Museum pins a small fraction of them back onto the city they came from: one photo, one place, one short story at a time.

Turn the year layers on and the city ages in front of you. A gate in 1905. The same street in the 1980s. Your corner last winter. The museum grows whenever someone adds a photo, and every entry is read by a reviewer before it joins the map.

How to contribute

  • Choose a photo you took in Beijing and own the right to share.
  • Write the short story that goes with it, a few sentences are plenty.
  • Place it on the map and give it a year, your best guess is fine.
  • A reviewer checks the story, the place and the photo, then publishes it.

Privacy

No account is needed and no personal data is stored beyond what you type. Location metadata inside photo files is stripped on upload; the only location kept is the point you place on the map. Names are optional. People in photos must have agreed to appear or be unidentifiable.

Maps and sources

The base map is © OpenStreetMap contributors, tiles by CARTO. Historical era overlays draw on digitized maps of the city, including the Qianlong-era Complete Map of Peking (1750) and Frank Dorn's pictorial map of Peiping (1936). Founding seed photographs come from public-domain collections of old Beijing, credited on each entry.

Built as an open, local-first project. Every entry is reviewed before publication, following community-moderation practice from open platforms like Ushahidi, and the photo-on-a-map tradition of OldNYC.
